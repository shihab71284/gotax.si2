IRS notice explained · CP11

We changed your return — refund adjustment

The IRS made a change to your return and the result affects your refund amount.

low Typical deadline: No response needed unless you disagree (60-day dispute window is common) aka CP-11, Refund adjustment
EnglishAuthoritative

What this notice is

A CP11 tells you the IRS adjusted something on your return — often math, a credit, or an offset — and shows the new refund amount.

  • Common causes: math-error correction, a credit disallowed, or a refund offset to another debt.

What the IRS is asking

Usually nothing is required from you if you agree. If you disagree, you must respond within the window shown on the notice (often 60 days).

  • Call the number on the notice, or write to the address shown, explaining why the adjustment is wrong.

Deadline and what happens if you miss it

There is generally no payment deadline, but the dispute window is limited.

  • Missing the dispute window usually means the adjustment stands.
  • If the refund was offset, the notice names the agency that received it.

Your options (pay, agree, disagree, request more time)

  • AGREE — no action needed; keep the notice with your tax records.
  • DISAGREE — respond in writing within the window with documents that support your position.
  • OFFSET — if a state or federal debt took the refund, contact that agency; the IRS cannot reverse it.

Documents you may need

  • Your filed return, the refund transcript, and any offset notice (TOP).
  • Documents supporting the credit or figure the IRS changed.

Recommended next steps

  • 1. Compare the IRS adjustment with your filed return line by line.
  • 2. Check whether it is a math error, a disallowed credit, or an offset.
  • 3. If it is an offset, confirm which agency and amount.
  • 4. Respond before the dispute window closes if you disagree.

Questions to ask a tax professional

  • Which line of my return changed and why?
  • Is this a math-error adjustment, and can I still dispute it under that procedure?
  • Was my refund offset, and to which agency?
