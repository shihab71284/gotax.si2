IRS notice explained · CP14

Balance due — you owe tax

The IRS calculated that you still owe tax, penalties and interest for this tax year.

medium Typical deadline: 21 days from the notice date (30 days if you owe less than $100,000 and the notice is addressed outside the US) aka CP-14, Balance Due, Notice of balance due
EnglishAuthoritative

What this notice is

A CP14 is a balance-due notice. It means the IRS processed your return, applied your payments and withholding, and shows a remaining balance of tax, penalty and interest.

  • It is a bill, not an audit and not a proposed change.
  • It usually arrives weeks after you filed, and it lists the amounts line by line.

What the IRS is asking

The IRS wants the full balance paid by the date shown on the notice.

  • Pay online (Direct Pay), by card through a processor, or by check with the voucher.
  • If you cannot pay in full, still file a response or set up an instalment agreement — ignoring it makes the problem worse.

Deadline and what happens if you miss it

The deadline is normally 21 days from the notice date (or 30 days for smaller balances). The date on your notice controls.

  • After the deadline, the failure-to-pay penalty and interest continue to accrue.
  • Unpaid balances can move to collection: CP501 reminder, CP504 intent to levy, then a final notice with your right to a hearing.

Your options (pay, agree, disagree, request more time)

  • PAY IN FULL — by the due date to stop further penalties and interest.
  • SET UP A PAYMENT PLAN — an Online Payment Agreement (streamlined for balances within the limits) or Form 9465.
  • DISPUTE THE BALANCE — if you already paid or the math is wrong, send proof of payment and a written explanation.
  • REQUEST PENALTY ABATEMENT — first-time penalty abatement or reasonable cause, in writing, after the balance is resolved.

Documents you may need

  • Your filed return and the payment/withholding records (W-2 box 2, 1099 withholding, Form 1040-ES vouchers).
  • Bank or card statements proving payment, confirmation numbers, IRS account transcript.
  • The CP14 itself, plus any earlier notices for the same tax year.

Recommended next steps

  • 1. Verify the balance on your IRS online account — it may already reflect a payment in transit.
  • 2. Check the penalty and interest lines: they are calculated, so an error usually means the underlying tax figure is wrong.
  • 3. Pay what you can before the deadline, then request an instalment agreement for the rest.
  • 4. Keep the confirmation number for every payment.
  • 5. If you believe the balance is wrong, respond in writing with proof before the deadline.

Questions to ask a tax professional

  • Does my IRS account transcript match this notice?
  • Am I eligible for first-time penalty abatement?
  • Which payment plan fits my cash flow, and what is the user fee?
  • Is part of this balance from a prior year or a state offset?
  • Could an offer in compromise or currently-not-collectible status apply?
