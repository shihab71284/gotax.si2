Frequently Asked Questions About Disputing IRS Notices

How do I respond to an IRS CP2000 notice if I disagree?

To disagree with an IRS CP2000 notice, check the "I do not agree with all or part of the proposed changes" box on the response form, attach a formal written statement explaining why the IRS calculation is incorrect (e.g. missing 1099-B cost basis or duplicate 1099-NEC), include supporting documents, and mail or fax it to the address listed on the notice within 30 days (60 days if outside the US).

Source: IRS Publication 5181 • Verified: November 2026

What happens if I do not respond to an IRS notice within 30 days?

If you fail to respond to a CP2000 or 30-day notice within the statutory window, the IRS will automatically assess the proposed additional tax, penalties, and interest, and issue a Statutory Notice of Deficiency (CP3219A / 90-day letter). At that stage, your only administrative option is filing a petition with the United States Tax Court.

Source: Internal Revenue Code § 6213(a) • Verified: November 2026

What is IRS First-Time Penalty Abatement (FTA)?

First-Time Penalty Abatement (FTA) is an administrative waiver provided under Internal Revenue Manual (IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1) that allows eligible taxpayers to remove failure-to-file and failure-to-pay penalties if they have a clean compliance record with no penalties for the preceding 3 tax years.

Source: IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1 • Verified: November 2026
